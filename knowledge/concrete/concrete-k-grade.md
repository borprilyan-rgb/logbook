## Quick Answer

K grade is a traditional Indonesian concrete designation associated with compressive strength measured on cube specimens, commonly expressed in kgf/cm². Always confirm the specimen, test age, and acceptance criteria in the project specification.

## Working Knowledge

A designation such as **K-350** is different from a specification using **f′c in MPa**, which generally refers to cylinder strength. Converting units alone does not convert cube strength into cylinder strength.

## Practical QS Notes

- Read the structural specification before selecting a concrete rate.
- Check whether supply includes pumping, testing, admixtures, and placement.
- Keep strength grade, exposure requirements, and slump as separate specification items.
- Request a written clarification when drawings and BOQ use inconsistent designations.

## Example

A BOQ calls for K-350 while a supplier quotes f′c 30 MPa. Do not assume equivalence. Ask the designer to confirm the required performance and test basis before comparing rates.
