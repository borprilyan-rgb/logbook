## Quick Answer

Slump measures the subsidence of fresh concrete after a standard cone is lifted. It helps assess consistency; it does not directly measure compressive strength.

## Working Knowledge

The required slump depends on the mix, reinforcement congestion, placement method, and project specification. Record the measured value alongside the batch, location, and time. Follow the specified test procedure and acceptance range.

## Practical QS Notes

- Check whether pumping and workability requirements affect the concrete rate.
- Keep delivery tickets and test records linked to the pour.
- Uncontrolled addition of water can affect performance and acceptance.
- Self-compacting concrete may require a different consistency test.

## Example

If a delivery falls outside the specified slump range, follow the approved quality procedure. A QS should record any rejected load, delay, or additional cost and check its contractual treatment.
