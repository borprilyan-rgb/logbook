## Quick Answer

A **control joint** creates a planned location for shrinkage cracking. An **expansion joint** provides separation to accommodate movement between adjoining elements.

## Working Knowledge

Control joints may be formed or saw-cut according to the design. Expansion joints may include compressible filler, sealant, and other specified components. Isolation joints separate an element from restraints; terminology can vary between drawings and specifications.

## Practical QS Notes

- Measure each joint type separately according to the applicable measurement rules.
- Check width, depth, sealant, backing rod, filler, and edge preparation.
- Confirm whether dowels or waterstops are included in the joint rate or separate items.
- Never infer spacing or cutting depth from a generic detail.

## Example

A slab joint rate may include saw-cutting and sealant, while an expansion joint rate also needs full-depth filler and a specific movement detail. Check the drawings before treating the two items as equivalent.
